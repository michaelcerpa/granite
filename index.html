AI for a regulated bank

An AI assistant a bank can trust.

Granite is a concept, not a product. It shows how I'd design an assistant for a large bank. Pick a question and follow it through the system. When it isn't sure, it refuses instead of guessing.

Two problems

Why it exists, and why it's built this way

The first problem is why it should exist. The second shaped how it's built.

A · The user's problem

Too many systems

A routine answer can live in a new platform, an old system, or both. It sits behind menus, and each system names things differently. Every lookup costs time and leaves people unsure they found the right thing.

  • Several steps across several systems
  • The same thing has different names in different places
B · The bank's problem

Regulated and old

These two facts shaped the whole design.

  • Regulated: a wrong answer is a compliance problem. Who can see what is controlled, and everything is logged.
  • Old: the data is spread across legacy systems, and you can't rebuild them.
The design · follow a question

Why it matters

Match the tool to the risk

It solves a real problem inside a bank's rules without risking a wrong answer.

Restraint

The router follows fixed rules. Figures come from the system of record. When the system isn't sure, it refuses.

Tradeoffs

It refuses more often. It follows rules where it could improvise. No person reviews answers as they go out. Each is the right trade for a bank.

What's not done

What I'd harden next: keep re-tuning the confidence thresholds, show figures without the AI touching them, and control for bias in the second model.
